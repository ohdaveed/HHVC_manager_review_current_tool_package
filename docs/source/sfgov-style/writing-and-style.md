# SF.gov writing and style guidance

Captured 2026-07-30 from the SF.gov and Karl Editor Help Center. Each section
cites the page it came from. See `README.md` for provenance and for the
conflicts with `notebooklm/hhvc-standards-manual.md` that are deliberately left
unresolved.

`✓ scored` marks a rule `js/plain-language.js` checks today. `— not checked`
marks one it deliberately does not, with the reason.

## Content principles

Source: `writing-on-sf.gov/content-principles`

1. **Start with people.** "Talk to your readers directly. Meet them where they
   are and tell them what they need to do." Use the language your audience uses.
2. **Make it easy.** "Hide the complexity of our departments and processes. Only
   give users the information they need at that time."
3. **Write for everyone.** "Write simply. Use plain English." Aim for a 5th-grade
   reading level, checked with the Hemingway Editor. Plan translations alongside
   English updates.
4. **Engage communities.** Work with San Franciscans who find City services hard
   to use.
5. **Earn trust.** "Keep the website current, accurate and the single source of
   truth."
6. **Hold ourselves to account.** Review every page annually, more often for
   popular ones.
7. **Digital first.** "Prioritize digital, so San Franciscans can help themselves
   when and where it's best for them" — while still saying clearly how to get
   in-person help.

## How people read online

Source: `sf.gov-and-karl-foundations/writing-for-sf.gov/reading-online`

"Almost all people skim instead of read online." A Nielsen Norman Group study
found readers take in roughly **20% of the words** on a page, and the page notes
that 2008 research probably understates how efficiently people skim now. They
scan because "they are looking for a specific piece of information" and "don't
want to read an entire page to find what they need."

The consequence: "your reader only needs to know what they need to do. And
almost nothing else." Use bulleted lists, descriptive subheadings, short
sentences in short paragraphs, one point per paragraph, and put the important
information at the top.

## Readability

Source: `writing-on-sf.gov/write-your-content/readability`

- **Reading level:** 5th grade, measured in the Hemingway Editor. A guideline,
  not a publication gate.
- **Sentence length:** "Users understand 100% of sentences at 9 words or less",
  and 90% at 14. ✓ scored (mean ≤ 15 words, outliers over 20 flagged)
- **Paragraph length:** 3 sentences or fewer. ✓ scored
- **Subheadings:** roughly every 3 paragraphs. ✓ scored
- **Lists:** convert to bullets when there are more than 3 items. ✓ scored
- **Verbs:** use strong verbs that show action — test with the prefix
  "Now you can…".
- **Voice:** active, not passive. ✓ scored
- **Positive framing:** prefer positive language over negatives like "cannot"
  and "do not".

## Plain language

Source: `sf.gov-and-karl-foundations/writing-for-sf.gov/plain-language/how-to-write-in-plain-language`

- Aim for **fewer than 10 words** per sentence; break at connectors (and, but,
  or). "Short bullets add white space."
- Fewer than 3 sentences per paragraph. "White space makes your page easy to
  scan." Written for readers with dyslexia and non-native English speakers.
- "Use short, simple, and common words." Avoid jargon. ✓ scored
- **Avoid contractions** — "readers miss them while scanning". ✓ scored
- **"You"** for the reader, **"we"** for the department. ✓ scored
- Active voice. Test: add "by zombies" to the sentence; if it still parses, it
  is passive. ✓ scored
- "It's always more important to be clear than to follow SF.gov style rules."

## Tone and voice

Source: `writing-on-sf.gov/write-your-content/tone-and-voice`

- Address readers as "you"; refer to the department as "we" or "us".
- Write conversationally, as you would speak to a real person.
- **Be direct and authoritative rather than deferential.** Do not say "please"
  when telling San Franciscans what they need to do. ✓ scored
- Use "you" and "we" to avoid gendered language; otherwise "they/their".
  - Write: "If you are under 18 years old, you must have a court order to get
    married."
  - Not: "If the bride is under 18, she must obtain…"
- Name the specific department if "we" would be ambiguous. "Clarity is more
  important than following the rules."

## Page structure

Sources: `…/writing-for-sf.gov/page-structure/*`

- **Front-load titles.** "Have the most important and distinct words at the
  beginning of your page title. Make it clear what people can do on the page."
  ✓ scored (headings opening with "How to", "Instructions for", …)
- **Headings.** H2 for page titles, H3 for subheadings, kept in consistent order
  for screen readers. "Write clear, simple headings to anchor your text." Start
  with strong verbs and lead with keywords.
  **Questions are not allowed as headings** — "FAQs are harder for people to
  understand and slower to read." ✓ scored
- **Break up your text.** Headings, bullets, short paragraphs, white space.
  "Avoid writing walls of text. They don't get read." ✓ scored
- **Just enough information.** Give people what they need for the task, nothing
  more.

## Inclusive writing

Source: `writing-on-sf.gov/write-your-content/be-inclusive`

- **Disability:** avoid "disabled", "handicapped", "confined to a wheelchair",
  and stigmatising terms ("crazy", "dumb", "lame", "insane", "psycho",
  "schizophrenic", "stupid"). Avoid sensory clichés ("blind spot", "tone deaf").
  Say "has multiple sclerosis", not "suffers from" or "afflicted with".
- **Age:** do not mention age unless relevant. Prefer "older person" over
  "senior" or "elderly"; use specific ranges where possible.
- **Gender:** gender-neutral language; "they/them" when pronouns are unknown;
  "spouse/partner" not "husband/wife"; "parent" not "mother/father"; avoid "guys"
  for mixed groups. Use identity terms as modifiers ("a transgender person"), not
  nouns.
- **Immigration status:** "Do not use the word citizen as a generic term for
  people who live in the United States." Use "San Franciscan"; reserve "citizen"
  for where citizenship is actually required.
- **Race and ethnicity:** avoid "non-white" and stereotypes. "Use adjectives, not
  nouns (for example, a Hispanic person, not a Hispanic)." Ask how people
  identify rather than assuming.

## Translation readiness

Source: HHVC standards manual §7.7, consistent with the content principles above

Vital information must reach Spanish, Chinese, and Filipino (Vietnamese
planned). Simple sentence structure translates more accurately.

- **No idioms, slang, puns, or cultural references.** ✓ scored
- **Consistent terminology** — never swap "Notice of Violation" for "citation
  notice" or "warning ticket". Translators cannot reconcile synonyms, and in an
  enforcement context that is a legal error. ✓ scored

| Oxford comma | Use it: "apples, bananas, and pears". | — not checked |
| Contractions | Fine in news and lighter content; avoid negative contractions in service and instructional copy. | ✓ scored (banned — HHVC pages are service copy) |
| Dashes | **Avoid dashes entirely.** Hyphens for compounds ("10-minute delay", "in-law unit"); spell out ranges ("Monday to Friday"). | ✓ scored |
| Dates | "January 28, 2026" or "Jan 28, 2026". No slashes, no ordinals ("28", not "28th"). | ✓ scored |
| Ellipses | Do not use — "they cause confusion". | ✓ scored (verbatim code quotes exempt) |

| Latin | No "i.e.", "e.g.", "etc." — "not easily understood by everyone". Use "like", "for example", or be specific. | ✓ scored |

| Nonprofit | One word, no hyphen. | ✓ scored |
| Numbered lists | For sequential steps only. Periods at the end of each item. | — not checked |
| Numbers | "Always use numbers, even for 1 to 9." Spell out at the start of a sentence or where digits would confuse. | — not checked (too many legitimate exceptions) |

## Pre-publication QA checklist

Source: `writing-on-sf.gov/write-your-content/check-your-content`

- Who is the user? What do they want to do — **one thing per page**? When is it
  done (acceptance criteria)?
- Does the first sentence explain what the service does? Does every sentence
  help with the task?
- Titles **65 characters**, descriptions **110**. Sentence case. Check for
  stray capitalisation.
  *(Note the conflict: the HHVC manual says a 60-character title and a 110–160
  character description. See README.)*
- Sentences under 15 words; paragraphs 3 sentences or fewer; a subheading about
  every 3 paragraphs; long inline lists converted to bullets.
- Images have alt text. No text inside images.
- No "click here" link text.

## SF.gov's own AI guidelines

Source: `writing-on-sf.gov/ai-guidelines`

Directly relevant to the AI assist feature, and consistent with HHVC standards
manual §1.11:

1. **"Always fact check AI-generated content before using it."**
2. **"Always disclose usage of Generative AI in your output."**
3. **"Never enter sensitive information into public Generative AI tools, like
   ChatGPT."** Because "the information you enter can be viewed by the companies
   that make the tools, and in some cases, members of the public."

Content intended for the public is not treated as sensitive, so draft page copy
may be processed. Digital Services also notes that the free Hemingway Editor
tier does not use AI, and recommends it for readability checks.

**Implication for this tool:** generated output must be labelled as AI-assisted
wherever it is exported or copied, and the feature must never be able to mark a
page approved — HHVC standards manual §1.11: "No automated agent may
programmatically set a page status to 'Approved to Move = Yes.'"

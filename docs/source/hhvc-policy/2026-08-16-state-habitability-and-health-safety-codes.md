## 1. Statutory Foundations of Habitability in California

California state law establishes non-waivable minimum habitability standards for all residential rental dwelling units. Local municipal health and housing codes (including San Francisco Health Code Article 11) operate as local enforcement mechanisms concurrent with state law.

### California Civil Code § 1941.1 — Standard Characteristics of a Tenantable Dwelling
A residential dwelling is deemed untenantable under California law if it substantially lacks any of the following standard characteristics:
1. **Weatherproofing & Weather Protection:** Effective waterproofing and weather protection of roof and exterior walls, including unbroken windows and doors.
2. **Plumbing Facilities:** Plumbing or gas facilities that conform to applicable law in effect at the time of installation, maintained in good working order.
3. **Water Supply & Sewage:** A water supply approved under applicable law that produces hot and cold running water, with fixtures connected to an approved sewage disposal system.
4. **Heating Facilities:** Heating facilities that conform with applicable law at the time of installation, maintained in good working order.
5. **Electrical Lighting & Wiring:** Electrical lighting, with wiring and electrical equipment that conforms with applicable law, maintained in good working order.
6. **Cleanliness & Vermin Exclusion:** Building, grounds, and appurtenances at the time of the commencement of the lease or rental agreement, and all areas under control of the landlord, kept clean, sanitary, and free from all accumulations of debris, filth, rubbish, garbage, rodents, and vermin.
7. **Refuse Receptacles:** An adequate number of appropriate receptacles for garbage and rubbish, in clean condition and good repair.
8. **Floors, Stairways, & Railings:** Floors, stairways, and railings maintained in good repair.
9. **Deadbolt Locks & Window Security:** Approved deadbolt locks on exterior doors and functioning window security devices.

## 2. California Health & Safety Code § 17920.3 — Substandard Buildings

Under California Health & Safety Code § 17920.3, any building or portion thereof in which there exists any of the following conditions to an extent that endangers the life, limb, health, property, safety, or welfare of the public or the occupants thereof is declared to be a **Substandard Building**:

### Inadequate Sanitation (§ 17920.3(a))
- Lack of, or improper water closet, lavatory, or bathtub/shower in a dwelling unit.
- Lack of, or improper water closets, lavatories, and bathtubs/showers per number of guests in a hotel.
- Lack of, or improper kitchen sink.
- Lack of hot and cold running water to plumbing fixtures in a dwelling unit or hotel.
- Lack of adequate heating.
- Lack of, or improper operation of required ventilating equipment.
- Lack of minimum amounts of natural light and ventilation required by code.
- Room and space dimensions less than required by code.
- Lack of required electrical lighting.
- **Dampness of habitable rooms (including visible mold or continuous moisture).**
- **Infestation of insects, vermin, or rodents as determined by the health officer or code enforcement agency.**
- **General dilapidation or improper maintenance.**
- **Lack of connection to required sewage disposal system.**
- **Lack of adequate garbage and rubbish storage and removal facilities.**

### Structural Hazards (§ 17920.3(b))
- Deteriorated or inadequate foundations.
- Defective, deteriorated, or inadequate floor or wall supports.
- Deteriorated, defective, or inadequate ceiling, roof, or fireplace supports.

### Hazardous Wiring & Mechanical Equipment (§ 17920.3(c), (d))
- Wiring that does not conform to all applicable laws in effect at the time of installation or that has not been maintained in good condition.
- Equipment or piping that does not conform to safety standards.

## 3. Statutory Protections Against Landlord Retaliation (Civil Code § 1942.5)

Under California Civil Code § 1942.5 and San Francisco Rent Ordinance § 37.9(a)(8):
1. **Unlawful Retaliation Prohibited:** It is unlawful for a lessor or property owner to increase rent, decrease services, cause a lessee to quit involuntarily, bring an action to recover possession, or threaten to do any of those acts for the purpose of retaliating against the lessee because the lessee has:
   - Lawfully organized or participated in a tenants' association or group.
   - Complained to the appropriate agency (such as DPH or DBI) concerning tenantability or code violations.
   - Exercised any rights under the law.
2. **180-Day Protection Window:** When a tenant makes an oral or written complaint to an agency or landlord regarding housing conditions, a landlord cannot take retaliatory action within 180 days of the inspection, citation, or resolution.
3. **Damages for Retaliation:** Landlords who engage in retaliatory eviction or harassment are liable for actual damages, punitive damages, and attorney's fees under state and local law.

## 4. Relationship Between State Law and SF Health Code Article 11

- **Local Authority:** San Francisco Health Code Article 11 acts as the municipal enforcement codification of Health & Safety Code § 17920.3's sanitation, insect, rodent, and nuisance provisions within the City and County of San Francisco.
- **Enforcement Priority:** DPH Environmental Health inspectors cite municipal Health Code Article 11 directly in administrative Notices of Violation (NOVs), while referencing state substandard building statutes (§ 17920.3) when escalating cases to Director's Hearings, Orders of Abatement, or City Attorney civil litigation.
